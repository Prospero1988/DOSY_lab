# Diffusion alignment and Adaptive Local ILT for DOSY maps

Open **2D DOSY map** after completing the integrals and fits. Use **Map mode** to compare the following views. **Original DOSY map** is the default and keeps each reference spectrum segment at its own monoexponential D.

- **D-aligned DOSY · display only** moves the existing reference segments onto candidate group D values. Within an accepted group, the representative is the inverse-variance weighted mean when all $\mathrm{SE}(D)$ values are finite and positive, otherwise the median. Peak positions in ppm, spectral profiles and intensities are retained.
- **Global fit DOSY** fits one positive D across all included ROI decays of each candidate group. Each ROI has an independent amplitude. Check the group tree for original and shared D, and hover over each ROI for independent and shared residual RMS. An incompatible group keeps its independent D values. Clear a member checkbox to remove it from the fit and regroup the remaining ROIs. The group checkbox controls only map visibility.
- **Global ILT DOSY · ROI profiles** becomes available after a Global ILT calculation converges for the same processing and selected ROIs. For a single retained component, the map places the reference profile at its ILT D only after checking that the plotted D explains the measured attenuation and no positive model mass was omitted. For multiple components, it estimates separate profiles from the measured spectra across the active gradient layers. This mode can preserve multiple components at overlapping ppm within one ROI.
- **Adaptive Local ILT** builds compatible groups from the cached ROI fits and solves a joint ILT independently for each group. It is available directly on the map and does not require an earlier Global ILT calculation.

Adjust **D tolerance** with the slider or the exact numeric field. The range is 1–30% and starts at 5%. **Recalculate alignment** reapplies the settings. **Reset to original** switches to the original map and restores the full view. Use **Z** for zoom, **P** for pan and **F** for full range. The wheel over **D projection** changes horizontal intensity scaling. PNG exports identify the active method and indicate how many ROIs were processed in a non-original mode.

## Clustering and shared fit

Grouping considers fits with positive finite D, at least five active points, $R^2$ between 0.95 and 1, and $\mathrm{SE}(D)$ at most 25% of D when a finite SE is available. These thresholds screen candidates for alignment. In increasing D order, a candidate joins the current cluster only if its symmetric distance from the smallest D in that cluster is within tolerance. The distance is $200\lvert D_1-D_2\rvert/(D_1+D_2)$ percent. This bounds the distance between every pair of members and prevents chain linking. Singleton ROIs retain their own D.

The shared fit uses the original b, signed integrals and fit masks. Each column is divided by its largest absolute active integral. When the monoexponential fit used integral SE, the shared fit preserves its relative inverse-SE weights. Residual weights have unit $L_2$ norm per ROI. At a trial D, each nonnegative amplitude is determined analytically, with its reference at the lowest active b. A bounded scalar minimization fits the shared D. A candidate is rejected if any ROI's normalized residual RMS exceeds both $`1.5\times\mathrm{RMS}_{\mathrm{independent}}`$ and $`\mathrm{RMS}_{\mathrm{independent}}+0.005`$. This is a screening heuristic. Tighter tolerance or manual membership can help isolate incompatible decays. Similar D or lower noise in a shared fit cannot establish molecular identity.

## ILT component profiles

Each candidate component uses the decay basis implied by its entire ILT interval, normalized at the displayed reference layer. A single retained component must pass separate checks for the fitted interval and the exponential at the plotted maximum, using the independent-fit residual screen described above. A material dependence of D on the ROI shape or phase can prevent a single-component assignment. The map screens spectral mismatch above the expected measurement noise energy. Its 15% relative threshold applies to the excess RMS. This remains a heuristic and the reference profile needs S/N of at least five. Multicomponent reconstruction also requires a basis condition number of at most 100 and agreement within 20 percentage points between reconstructed and ILT reference shares. Contributions below 5% at the reference layer can participate in the spectral solve as nuisance terms, with their amplitude excluded from the displayed bands. If only one component remains after dropping positive mass, the ROI remains unresolved.

The default overlay highlights unresolved measured profiles in amber on the 1D reference spectrum. The map selector also offers an assigned-only view or optional dashed guides at the independent monoexponential D, when available. Guides carry no reconstructed intensity and do not enter the D projection. Choose an ROI in the sidebar to inspect its rejection reason and available metrics. The display reports counts and warns when a floor hides assigned signals. PNGs include the active method and overlay legend.

The method only operates inside selected ROIs. Points shared by overlapping ROI definitions remain masked in the display. The Gaussian width along D remains a display setting. The main fit table, source spectra and saved projects retain their original measurements and fit results.

## Adaptive Local ILT

1. Select **Adaptive Local ILT** in the map. Adjust **D tolerance**, **Amplitude balance** and **Local λ** independently. The defaults are 5%, 10× and $10^{-6}$. **Restore defaults** restores those three values. Edits trigger a background solve after a short pause.
2. Inspect **Group diagnostics** for the ROI count, monoexponential D range, dominant recovered D, amplitude ratio, normalized reconstruction RMS, convergence and unresolved ROIs. Select a group in the sidebar to highlight its bands. Use the group checkbox to hide its bands and the ROI checkbox to exclude an ROI from regrouping.
3. Compare with **Original DOSY map** and, where useful, with **Global ILT DOSY** from a manual selection. A tight local tolerance separates the inversions; it cannot establish that two nearby populations are experimentally distinguishable.

The complete-link D rule proposes candidates. The shared-fit RMS check described above removes incompatible members and retries compatible subsets. The solver also requires six distinct active b values and a positive reference integral with an estimated S/N of at least five. It uses a valid reference SE when supplied and the monoexponential residual RMS as a proxy otherwise. Residual RMS is a screening proxy and does not estimate independent noise.

**Amplitude balance** is the ratio of the largest positive reference integral in a group to another ROI's reference integral. Every curve is independently normalized even below the threshold. Above it, an ROI with lower estimated S/N can receive a smaller residual weight, bounded below by 0.25 of the unit weight. Complete positive point SE vectors additionally supply within-ROI weights. Amplitude never creates a diffusion group by itself. **Local λ** is the zeroth-order regularization strength. Each group uses a 100-cell logarithmic grid, extending 20-fold below and above its monoexponential estimates, and joint sparsity $\mu=10^{-4}$ within that group. The original b, signs, masks and intensity units are preserved for reconstruction.

An ungrouped ROI with nontrivial monoexponential residuals can be split independently if two nearby $\lambda$ settings give consistent interior maxima, the residual improves by at least 20%, and the per-bin reconstruction passes the profile checks above. A recovered component is associated with a group for visibility when exactly one group's dominant D falls within tolerance. Each component keeps its own recovered D and is rendered once. Multicomponent profiles partition the positive reference intensity across components when all modeled mass is retained. When modeled mass is omitted, the retained profiles are not renormalized to the whole reference. If unrepresented bins contain more than 1% of the positive reference sum, or the partition fails the residual or amplitude checks, the ROI remains unresolved. Overlapping ROI definitions still use the existing shared-bin mask.

In a synthetic mixture with true D of `3.00`, `3.25` and `9.00` in units of $`10^{-10}\ \mathrm{m^2/s}`$, independent monoexponential fits gave distinct estimates. Whole-selection ILT returned two maxima near `3.03` and `9.44`. Adaptive Local ILT returned three group maxima near `3.07`, `3.35` and `8.73`, including a reliable ROI 100 times weaker than its group partner. The noise-dominated ROI was left unresolved. In an overlapping-resonance fixture, two profiles were reconstructed near `1.91` and `16.49` for true D of `2` and `16`. Their sum reproduced the positive reference profile. These are fixture results for the tested b coverage, S/N and settings.
